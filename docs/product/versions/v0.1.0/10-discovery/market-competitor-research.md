# Market and Competitor Research

## Current alternatives

Linear is the direct reference and proprietary incumbent for the observed workflow. Plane advertises open-source work items, cycles, modules, views, pages, and analytics under AGPL-3.0. OpenProject provides a GPL-3.0 Community Edition with broad planning, agile, time, cost, wiki, and meeting capabilities. Taiga uses MPL-2.0 for an open-source agile platform. Vikunja offers an owned, self-hosted task manager under AGPL-3.0-or-later for most code. GitHub Projects is a strong partial substitute when repository work dominates.

## Strong substitutes

Plane is the closest open-source product-position substitute because it explicitly addresses modern issue, cycle, view, page, and roadmap workflows. OpenProject is the strongest breadth and maturity substitute. Vikunja is a strong ownership and operational-simplicity substitute. Linear remains the strongest interaction reference.

## Replacement strategy

Build a focused clean-room product rather than orchestrating Linear. Do not fork a competitor during Discovery: broad codebases would accelerate feature count but inherit information architecture, styling, operational complexity, license obligations, and migration assumptions before user evidence justifies them. Planning may revisit library or source reuse only with explicit license and architecture analysis.

## Testable differentiation

Differentiation is accepted only if the product meets all four tests: core workflow completion, no more than the accepted median time/interaction overhead versus Linear, approved structural screenshot thresholds with independent branding, and complete self-host export/restore without external services. Feature presence alone does not pass.

## Switching cost

Known switching costs include data export and import, workflow/status mapping, project and milestone mapping, issue relations, rich text, attachments, saved views, keyboard relearning, self-host operations, and trust in backup/upgrade behavior. CT-77 ranks workflow families but does not measure exact import-format prevalence or switching effort. Those remain explicit residual risk; they do not justify multi-user identity or membership UX in v0.1.

## Weak evidence

Repository stars, vendor descriptions, and public feature lists do not establish adoption quality, migration success, performance, or user preference. No credible market-size or willingness-to-pay claim is made. “Free for everyone” is interpreted as free open-source availability. The maintainer selected `AGPL-3.0-only`; qualified release review remains owned by `CT-3` and `CT-13`.
